Arsenal Academy host script

Read the spoken lines. Follow the click cues. Keep the visuals on the classroom display.

The big page number and heading match the journal on the classroom display. Setup and optional Matchday are separate stops, not extra journal pages. Read SAY and ASK aloud; CLICK and PAUSE guide you. Keep this script on a second device or print it. Its controls move the script only.

Plan 45–60 minutes including discussion and 21 students choosing cards. Short route: take one answer per question, choose one Cinema film, skip Matchday and explore three scouting profiles. Card choosing sets the pace.

DOSet out 42 physical cards for 21 students. Know each student’s classroom number 1–21. Keep a paper checklist to confirm two cards per student. Decide who the six captains are before the ceremony; the Vault randomizes stories, not students.

DOPre-open A Champion’s Story, the 1:01 Merino trailer, on your presentation device. Check school access, volume and what appears before playback. Return to the Academy after it ends. The prior review covered available captions and 61 frames; it was not a complete audio listen. The owner accepted this trailer’s brief shirtless footage.

DONorth London Forever is the optional 27-second stadium clip you chose. Open it on your presentation device before class if using it. The two classroom cheers work without a recording.

DOUse a comfortable group voice. Children may join by speaking, gentle clapping, a hand motion or listening. Keep feet and hands in personal space.

NOTEOptional Matchday: read the date on the board. Its figures are a manually maintained snapshot, not live scores. For a later presentation, verify the official table before describing them as current.

NOTEPronunciation help: Arsène Wenger ≈ ar-SEN VENG-er; Mikel Merino ≈ MEE-kel meh-REE-noh; Saliba ≈ suh-LEE-buh. Names deserve care; model learning them together.

Journal 1 / 12 · THIS IS ARSENAL · 4 minutes

Journal page 1 This is Arsenal

CLICKStay in the clubhouse for the opening question. The next click opens journal page 1, THIS IS ARSENAL.

SAYAll right, important question. Possibly the most important question before lunch.

ASKWho do you think is the best football club in the world?

PAUSEInvite two or three students. If the room is quiet: “Arsenal? Real Madrid? Barcelona? Bayern Munich? Another team?” Accept their choices warmly.

SAYExcellent answers. And the correct answer, according to your extremely unbiased host, is… Arsenal! You may have detected a tiny amount of favoritism. You can absolutely support a different team. Today you get to discover my favorite.

ASKWho here likes soccer? Who has played? Who has watched a game or cheered for a team? Did anybody watch a World Cup? Who were you rooting for?

PAUSETake a few quick answers. Then ask “Favorite player—Messi, Ronaldo, or somebody else?” Welcome players from women’s and men’s football. No knowledge quiz or forced answer.

SAYYou already know something about being a teammate or supporter. We notice effort, cheer people on and help someone feel included. Today we are football explorers, scouts and card collectors. I have also promoted myself to stadium announcer. My contract lasts until the bell.

CLICKChoose ViewSonic Journal. Confirm the display says 1 / 12 and THIS IS ARSENAL.

SAYThis is Arsenal: a football club from London, England, founded in 1886. In England, soccer is usually called football. Same game, different word. Our Academy mission is to discover people and make sure everyone belongs.

DOAttention cue: you say “Team check!”; class answers “Ready!” and settles. Practise once at a comfortable volume.

Journal 2 / 12 · HOME IS NORTH LONDON · 2 minutes

Journal page 2 Home is North London

SAYHome is North London. This is Emirates Stadium, Arsenal’s home. Imagine a whole stadium cheering for the same team—all those people sharing one very strong opinion about where a ball should go.

ASKWhat makes a place feel like home? The building? The people? Someone saving you a seat?

PAUSETake two answers. Open “What makes a place feel like home?”

SAYA team is stronger when people feel welcome. Think about a big classroom job. If one person does everything, that can be a lot. When we share jobs and listen, we can often get more done and help each other through the tricky parts.

ASKWhat could a good leader do if someone is left out of the group?

SAYInvite them in. Explain the plan. Listen to their idea. Leadership can be as simple as “Come work with us.” Whether you love football, are brand-new to it or mostly came for the cards, you belong here.

DOOne gentle stadium wave across the room. Students can raise a hand or watch.

SAYBeautiful. Our stadium wave has excellent classroom management.

Journal 3 / 12 · THE INVINCIBLES · 2 minutes

Journal page 3 The Invincibles

SAYIn 2003–04, Arsenal played 38 Premier League games and lost zero. Zero! I have lost a pencil between taking attendance and putting the attendance sheet down.

SAYTheir manager was Arsène Wenger. They became the first team to complete a Premier League season unbeaten, and no other Premier League team has done it since. That is why this team is called the Invincibles.

NOTEHistorical distinction: Preston North End also went unbeaten in England’s top division in 1888–89, before the Premier League existed. Do not call Arsenal the first unbeaten English team ever. Claim checked against the Premier League archive, 19 November 2026.

ASKThey won 26 games and drew 12. A draw means a tied score. How many games is 26 plus 12? And how many defeats are left out of 38?

PAUSEOpen “26 wins + 12 draws. How many defeats?” after the class answers.

SAYThirty-eight games. Zero defeats. They did not win every game; they kept going together through the draws too. Being a great team takes more than one amazing afternoon. You prepare, help each other and try again.

DOInvite a quiet “wow” or a silent celebration.

CLICKNext page → 4 / 12.

NOTEJournal teacher note: Nobody beat Arsenal in the league that season. That’s why they were called the Invincibles.

Journal 5 / 12 · BELIEVE PREPARE IMPROVE · 2 minutes

Journal page 5 Believe Prepare Improve

SAYBelieve. Prepare. Improve. Meet Arsène Wenger—our visionary, which means someone who can imagine what is possible and help people work toward it. He managed Arsenal for 22 years, led the Invincibles, and won three Premier League titles and seven FA Cups.

SAYHe could not just stand on the sideline and yell “Be amazing!” for 90 minutes. A manager has to prepare the team, choose a plan and help players develop. Wenger helped his team become great through preparation and teamwork.

ASKYou are the coach. A teammate misses a chance and says “I can’t do it.” What could you say, and what could you help them practise?

PAUSETake a helpful phrase and a practical next step. Open “You’re the coach. What is your next move?”

SAY“Let’s try again. I can help you practise.” That is coaching we can use in football, math and during the mysterious disappearance of the glue-stick cap. Believing in someone means helping them take the next step.

Journal 7 / 12 · EVERY PLAYER MATTERS · 3 minutes

Journal page 7 Every player matters

SAYEvery player matters. Starting players, bench players, managers—everyone. You do not have to be the fastest runner or score the goal to help a team.

CLICKTouch Forward, Midfielder, Defender and Goalkeeper in turn.

SAYForwards find space and finish chances. Midfielders connect the team with passing and movement. Defenders protect the goal and build attacks. Goalkeepers make saves and can start the next attack. These are thinking jobs as well as moving jobs.

CLICKTouch Substitute.

SAYA substitute starts on the bench and may enter later. They watch, encourage and stay ready. A “super sub” is a nickname for a substitute who makes a big difference. Fewer minutes does not mean less importance.

SAYHere is Mikel Merino. He is usually a midfielder. Against Leicester on 15 February 2025, he came on in the 69th minute and played up front. He scored in the 81st and 87th minutes. Two late goals, and Arsenal won two–nil. His team needed a different job, and he stepped in. That bench seat was not the end of his story.

CLICKTouch Manager.

SAYManagers plan, encourage and help others improve. You can play, plan, encourage or help someone practise. You do not have to be on the pitch to belong or lead.

ASKWhen you are waiting for your turn in class, how can you still help the team? Tell a partner.

PAUSEGive 20 seconds; invite one answer. Listening, encouraging and staying ready all count.

Journal 11 / 12 · HOW WILL YOU LEAD · 3 minutes

Journal page 11 How will you lead

SAYHow will you lead? We have our cards. Now we need something you cannot keep in a plastic sleeve: leadership. Great captains help others, stay calm and make their team better.

CLICKOpen Tony Adams.

SAYTony Adams spent his whole senior playing career at Arsenal. He became captain at 21 and helped lead four league-title-winning teams. His story gives us loyalty and courage: keep showing up for your teammates, communicate and take responsibility when a job is difficult.

ASKSomeone is left out. What could a courageous teammate say?

PAUSEAccept “Join us” or “Let’s make room.”

CLICKOpen Sol Campbell.

SAYSol Campbell helped protect the goal in the Invincibles defense. A defender has to notice danger, communicate and keep making decisions under pressure. That gives us strength and composure. Composure means staying steady enough to think when things feel difficult.

ASKOur group makes a mistake. What would staying calm and helping look like?

PAUSEAccept taking a breath, listening and helping fix it together.

CLICKOpen Arsène Wenger.

SAYWenger led that unbeaten team as manager. His job was to see what people could become and help them prepare. That gives us vision and belief: share a plan, help someone practise and notice their progress. A good leader does not need every answer; they can ask for help too.

ASKWhich will you try today: invite someone in, stay calm or help someone improve?

SAYYou do not need a badge to begin. A captain badge is a reminder to help, not a license to become the boss of everybody’s pencils. Next we will open the Vault and discover each captain’s leadership story.

CLICKNext page → 12 / 12.

NOTEJournal teacher note: Great captains help others, stay calm, and make their team better.
